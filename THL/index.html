USO | Social campaign

Miss Peaches & Dave Portnoy

The right invitation made the first yes easy.

The USO's canine program brings therapy dogs to service members and their families, and the Canine Volunteer of the Year vote was a chance to grow awareness for that work. Dave Portnoy wasn't someone the organization was ready to approach directly, and he gets asks constantly. I saw that the most credible invitation was Miss Peaches. Her community and natural connection to a therapy-dog program made the idea feel true, not transactional. It didn't need to be a major campaign. It needed to be the right beginning.

The ask
Rally her followers to vote for the USO Canine Volunteer of the Year.
My role
Saw the opportunity, concepted and pitched it, secured the yes, and cleared and approved the videos through launch.
What it did
An unexpected spike: votes jumped from roughly 1,000 to more than 10,000, bringing new supporters closer to a program that changes lives. It was also a meaningful first experience for Dave, opening the door to future opportunities with him and Barstool Sports.

Prime Video | Base visit and release content

John Krasinski | Jack Ryan

Sincerity is what people remember.

Built around the release of the new season of Jack Ryan, the visit was designed to be meaningful for John and for the service members he met, and to be captured so the moment could be shared. Released across his channels and Prime Video's, the reach was significant, but what made it land was sincerity. People saw someone they already felt connected to show genuine care for service members.

The ask
Visit service members on base around the new season's release, with the experience shared across talent and studio channels.
My role
Built the partnership with Prime Video, researched locations, and pitched the day. Worked with the base to create an authentic, memorable experience with strong content opportunities, led planning and clearances, ran the day on site, and moved the content through approvals for a quick release.
What it did
Brought the USO to his audience in a way that felt genuine, because it came from him.

Studio partnerships

A model built on mutual value

They came back. That was the verdict.
MGMChanning Tatum | DOGThe campaign that established the model: two base visits, a virtual engagement with troops overseas, and a talent-signed donor campaign, each for a different audience.

I knew the USO could offer partners more than military access. It gave them a credible way to tell an American story about service, gratitude, and the values that still connect us. So I rebuilt the model around that value. Activations that once cost the USO $50,000 to $150,000 each became fully partner-funded, with studios bringing their talent and funding the experience. Their story and ours, told together.

The ask
Fund the activation and bring your talent to give service members an authentic experience that lifts morale and supports their well-being, then share that story with the public.
My role
Designed the model and every activation: built the day with each military installation, negotiated clearances, created the tour books, planned for strong visual content, and aligned teams across the organization so every visit produced a story both partners could use.
What it did
DOG became my proof. I used it to show other studios what the model could do, they chose to invest, and they came back. Partner-funded activations followed with Amazon MGM Studios, Prime Video, Netflix, Paramount+, and Focus Features.

Focus Features | Campaign pivot

PRESSURE | Letters Home

When the talent couldn't travel, the story still could.

Focus Features came to us wanting to go big: film talent visiting bases to meet service members and capture content. I saw a bigger opportunity. With morning shows dark during release week, I built a PRESSURE × Dunkin' × USO integration for Memorial Day, with a limited-edition cup and a 72-cent donation mirroring the film's 72 hours. It gave an American brand a genuine role in a patriotic moment, gave the public an easy way to give back, raised awareness for service members, and brought the D-Day story to life in a way that felt authentic to the film and the moment.

When the talent-led plan was no longer possible, I asked for a day and came back with Letters Home: a public archive of World War II letters from the USO's collection, digitized at no cost through Legacybox, paired with the organization's first creator mission immersion, fully funded by the studio. It gave the studio a story it could lean on at a sensitive time. The letters spoke to history lovers of every age, the same people drawn to the film, and the creators carried the story to younger audiences who might not know the USO, or D-Day.

The ask
Originally, bring film talent to bases to visit service members and capture content. In the end, Andrew Scott records a reading of a 1944 letter, and the studio funds 14 creators and media to take part in a USO mission immersion at Fort Campbell.
My role
Conceived both the Dunkin' integration and the Letters Home pivot, brought in Legacybox, coordinated the immersion with the military, and built the archive, the reading, and the experience with the studio.
What it did
The USO's first creator mission immersion, at no cost to the organization. A Washington Post feature on release day, coverage in Variety and Stars and Stripes, and a permanent public archive.
